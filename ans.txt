Why do you want to lead the Engineering Committee and what impact can you make?

As a fresher and SEP, I understand the initial gap that exists between new joiners and leadership. Many SEPs have strong ideas and energy but hesitate to communicate or are unsure how to create impact early on.

I want to lead the Engineering Committee to bridge this communication gap by creating more structured and approachable channels between SEPs and leadership, ensuring fresh perspectives are heard and valued.

I strongly believe that SEPs can make a significant impact at the firm when given the right exposure, guidance, and platform. As EC Lead, I would work towards increasing visibility of SEP contributions and encouraging active participation.

I plan to introduce new initiatives and programs such as leadership connect sessions, domain exploration talks, and peer-driven technical discussions to help SEPs learn faster and contribute more effectively.

On a personal level, this role gives me the opportunity to gain leadership exposure, develop stronger communication skills, and learn about different engineering and business domains within the firm.

I am motivated to take ownership, collaborate across teams, and ensure the Engineering Committee becomes a space where SEPs feel confident, connected, and empowered to grow and make meaningful contributions.



What ideas would you introduce to enhance community engagement and welcome new hires?

SEP Tank
A space where SEPs can pitch ideas, propose improvements, and work on problem statements, encouraging innovation and early ownership.

SEP Got Talent
A fun and inclusive initiative to showcase talents beyond work, helping SEPs connect with each other and build a strong community.

SEP Shift (Cross-Team Exposure Program)
A short, structured program where every SEP spends two weeks with a different team or LOB, similar to IP rotations. This helps SEPs understand how different teams function, gain broader exposure, and make more informed career decisions.

SEP Next (Technology & Hands-On Learning)
Interactive sessions introducing new and upcoming technologies, internal tools, and engineering practices across the firm, combined with hands-on labs where SEPs work on real scenarios to confidently apply these learnings in their day-to-day work.

Hands-On Labs & Guided Sessions
Practical learning sessions where SEPs work on real scenarios, helping them apply concepts confidently in their day-to-day work.


Describe your experience in organizing and volunteering for events

I have actively organized, hosted, and volunteered for multiple initiatives at JPMorgan Chase and during my college years, helping drive strong community engagement.

At JPMorgan Chase, I hosted Innovation Week across three locations, supporting planning, execution, and actively promoting sessions to increase participation. I engaged teams on the floor and used a roaming robo to spread awareness and encourage colleagues to attend Innovation Week sessions. I also hosted Ignite Spark, contributing to knowledge sharing within the engineering community.

I volunteered for the Tech for Social Good program, where I worked with NGOs to understand real-world challenges and explore how technology can create social impact.

This year, I was a speaker at PowerUp, representing my team and product, and I also set up a kiosk at Made in BTC Day to showcase our Innovation Week Global Hackathon–winning idea, engaging attendees and explaining the solution and its impact.

During my college years, I led the GeeksforGeeks coding club, where I helped build a strong coding culture by organizing regular technical sessions and mentoring students. I also focused on 1:1 interactions to ensure students felt supported and motivated to grow.
These experiences have strengthened my ability to engage people, coordinate events, and build inclusive communities.


How would you engage program engineers and ensure effective communication between tech communities and committees?

As an EC Lead, I would focus on creating structured, transparent, and inclusive communication for program engineers.

I would drive engagement through regular, purpose-driven interactions such as connect sessions and community forums, ensuring program engineers have clear opportunities to participate and contribute.

To ensure effective communication between tech communities and committees, I would establish clear communication channels and defined touchpoints, sharing regular updates and outcomes to maintain alignment and transparency.

I would also encourage representation of program engineers within committee discussions, enabling leadership to directly receive ground-level feedback.

Overall, my approach would be to bridge communication gaps, promote collaboration, and ensure consistent execution across communities and committees.
